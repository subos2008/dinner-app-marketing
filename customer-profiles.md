# Customer Profiles

## Launch Context
- **Phase 1:** Manchester (home turf, can iterate fast)
- **Phase 2:** London (large, English-speaking, high density of target profiles)
- **Phase 3:** Major US cities (NYC, LA, Chicago, SF, etc.)
- **Age range:** 25-35 primary, stretching to 45 as secondary
- **Large cities matter** — density of strangers, transient populations, higher loneliness rates

---

## Primary Segments

### 1. The Transplant
**Who:** Recently moved to the city (last 6-18 months). Has a job, maybe a flatmate, but no real social circle yet.

**Age skew:** 25-32

**Emotional state:** Optimistic but quietly frustrated. Thought they'd "just meet people" but it hasn't happened naturally. Starting to feel the gap between their busy work life and their empty weekends.

**Trigger:** A Sunday afternoon alone scrolling their phone, or declining a work social because they don't want to be the person who only socialises with colleagues.

**What they'd tell a friend:** "I know loads of people but I don't really *know* anyone here yet."

**Meta targeting signals:**
- Recently changed location (Meta tracks this)
- New job / company change
- Searching for "things to do in [city]"
- Following local event pages, food/restaurant accounts
- Age 25-32, single or in relationship (both valid)

**Messaging hook:** You moved here for a reason. Now meet the people who make it worth staying.

---

### 2. The Outgrower
**Who:** Has friends, but feels like they've plateaued. Their existing circle doesn't reflect where they're heading. Maybe they've started a business, got sober, had a kid, or just changed what they care about.

**Age skew:** 28-40

**Emotional state:** Not lonely in the traditional sense — more restless. They have Saturday night plans, but leave feeling like they had the same conversation again. Wants to be around people who challenge them.

**Trigger:** Leaving a gathering thinking "I love those people but I need something different." Or reading/listening to something inspiring and having nobody to talk to about it.

**What they'd tell a friend:** "I just want to meet people who are *doing* something, you know?"

**Meta targeting signals:**
- Follows self-improvement, business, wellness, or creative content
- Engages with podcasts (Diary of a CEO, Huberman, etc.)
- Interest in personal development, entrepreneurship, mindfulness
- Age 28-40
- May already attend events / networking — but wants something less transactional

**Messaging hook:** Your next chapter needs new characters.

---

### 3. The Quiet One
**Who:** Genuinely isolated. Doesn't have a social circle. Maybe never had a strong one, maybe it eroded over time. This is the person the product is *really* for, but they're the hardest to reach because they're the least likely to put themselves out there.

**Age skew:** 27-45

**Emotional state:** Resigned. Has probably tried things before (apps, meetups) and felt awkward or like they didn't belong. Protective of themselves. Needs to trust the process.

**Trigger:** A moment of honesty — maybe a long weekend alone, maybe seeing someone else's social life on Instagram, maybe just hitting a wall.

**What they'd tell a friend:** They wouldn't. That's the point.

**Meta targeting signals:**
- Harder to target directly — loneliness isn't an "interest"
- Passive scrollers, high screen time
- May follow mental health, wellbeing, introvert content
- Less likely to follow event/nightlife pages
- May respond to content rather than direct ads (story-driven, relatable, emotional)

**Messaging hook:** You don't have to be "a social person" to want connection. You just have to show up.

---

### 4. The Explorer
**Who:** Digital nomad or someone on an extended trip (1-6 months in a city). Has a laptop, a coworking membership or a cafe routine, and zero local friends. They chose freedom but didn't budget for how isolating it can be.

**Age skew:** 25-38

**Emotional state:** Loves the lifestyle, won't admit the trade-off. Instagram looks amazing — the reality is eating alone most nights in a city full of people they'll never speak to. Craves real conversation, not another hostel bar or coworking "networking drinks."

**Trigger:** Third dinner alone in a row. Or realising they've had more Slack conversations than real ones this week. The novelty of a new city wearing off and the loneliness settling in.

**What they'd tell a friend:** "It's amazing here, honestly. I just... wouldn't mind knowing some actual people."

**Meta targeting signals:**
- Interests: remote work, digital nomad, coworking, travel
- Follows accounts like Nomad List, Remote Year, travel creators
- Location doesn't match "hometown" — Meta knows this
- Recently arrived in city (similar to Transplant but temporary)
- May follow food/restaurant content in current city
- Age 25-38

**Messaging hook:** You didn't travel this far to eat alone.

**Why this segment matters:**
- High willingness to act — they're time-limited, so urgency is built in. No "I'll do it next month."
- Great word-of-mouth potential — nomads are networked online. One good experience gets shared in communities, Slack groups, Twitter/X.
- Natural repeat customers across cities — if we expand to multiple cities, this person books a dinner in every new place they land.
- Low friction — they're already used to showing up somewhere new and figuring it out. The social bravery barrier is lower.

---

## Secondary Segments (Future / Worth Watching)

### 5. The Life Changer
Post-breakup, post-divorce, new parent whose friend group shifted. Social circle disrupted by circumstance. Similar to The Transplant but didn't move — their life moved around them.

### 6. The Expat / International
Non-native English speaker in a new country. Extra isolation layer — cultural and linguistic. Huge in London and US cities. Could be a powerful niche.

## Segment Prioritisation for Meta Ads

| Segment | Ease of targeting | Willingness to act | Volume | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Transplant | High (location change data) | High (actively looking) | Medium | **#1 — Launch with this** |
| The Explorer | High (interest + location data) | High (time-limited, urgent) | Low-Medium | **#2 — High-value early adopter** |
| The Outgrower | Medium (interest-based) | Medium (needs the right nudge) | High | **#3 — Scale with this** |
| The Quiet One | Low (passive, hard to identify) | Low (high barrier) | High | **#4 — Content/brand play** |

---

## Implications for Ad Strategy

**The Transplant** is the launch segment. Easy to find on Meta, high intent, low persuasion needed. The product basically sells itself to this person — "dinner with 5 people who are also new here" is a no-brainer.

**The Explorer** is a high-value early adopter. Smaller pool but built-in urgency (they're leaving eventually), strong word-of-mouth in online nomad communities, and natural multi-city repeat potential as we expand. They're also easy to target — remote work and travel interests light up on Meta.

**The Outgrower** is the scale segment. Bigger pool, but needs more sophisticated messaging. They don't think of themselves as lonely — you have to frame it as growth, not fixing a problem.

**The Quiet One** won't respond to ads that say "meet new people!" — they've heard it before. This segment needs trust-building content: testimonials, stories, a brand that feels safe. Less performance marketing, more organic/brand.

## Open Questions

- Do we lead with "dinner" or "connection"? The Transplant cares about the activity. The Outgrower cares about the people. The Quiet One cares about the safety.
- Manchester-specific: any local cultural angles worth leaning into?
