## Overall Assessment

### Top 3 Strongest Ads

1. **The Frequency Shift — Variant B** (5/5) — The lead ad. "By 9pm, everyone else is on a different frequency. You're still on yours." is the most emotionally precise opening in the set. "Why the best part of going out is going home" is the line that will stop the scroll. The three-beat CTA — "No drift. No divergence. No exit strategy." — closes it perfectly.

2. **The Question Nobody Should Ask — Variant C** (5/5) — The emotional centrepiece. "Not the person who's into architecture. Not the person who ran three miles this morning. The person who doesn't drink." reframes the entire pain point. "Sober doesn't have to be the most interesting thing about you" is both a close and a manifesto.

3. **Sober Isn't the Headline — Variant B** (5/5) — The cleanest execution. The stacked negatives, the sourdough line, and "We don't celebrate your sobriety. We don't mention it. We just use it to match you with people who made the same choice, and then we get out of the way" is the best product description in the set.

### Top 3 Standout Lines

1. **"Nobody asks why you're not drinking. Because nobody's drinking."** — The entire campaign in two sentences. Works as a hook, a headline, or a billboard. Test as a standalone ad with product detail below the fold.

2. **"Stop explaining. Start eating."** — The punchiest CTA in the set. Mirrors the Transplant's "Stop googling. Start going." Has the same energy: acknowledges the exhaustion, then offers the action.

3. **"We don't celebrate your sobriety. We don't mention it. We just use it to match you with people who made the same choice, and then we get out of the way."** — The best product positioning in the set. Builds trust through restraint. This is the line that tells the audience "we actually understand you."

### Biggest Gap

**The empathy work surfaced a powerful grief angle — the loss of late-night honest conversations — that doesn't appear in the final ad copy.** From quick mode: "I lost the after-parties, the late-night conversations, the 2am kebab with someone who becomes a real friend because you were both drunk enough to be honest." This was Concept 6 and didn't make the top 4. It's the most emotionally complex angle: acknowledging that something real was lost alongside the hangovers, without romanticising drinking. A dedicated ad using this angle could be powerful for people further along in sobriety — those who aren't fragile but are grieving a specific kind of connection. It needs careful handling (could feel triggering for people in early recovery) but could be the highest-converting ad for the right sub-audience.

Additionally, the social proof voice is underused. The empathy journey's after-dinner scene — "Forty minutes in and nobody had mentioned alcohol. We talked about architecture." — is a strong first-person testimonial angle that could work as retargeting creative for people who've already seen the awareness ads.

### Rewrite Recommendations

No ads scored below 3. Specific fixes:

- **Frequency Shift A:** Tighten the product paragraph. "Not as a special accommodation. As the default." is the key line — cut the surrounding text to make it land harder. Current version: 4 sentences of product detail. Should be 2-3.
- **Frequency Shift C:** The CTA "Book a seat" doesn't match the emotional register of the rest of the ad. Consider: "A table where everyone's still here at 10pm." or "One dinner. Same frequency, start to finish."
- **Question Nobody Should Ask B:** Slightly long in the middle section. The "tonight" insight is gold but the paragraph around it could be one sentence shorter.
- **Sober Isn't the Headline A:** Runs slightly long. The list of what other sober events offer (raves, brunches, meet-ups) could be trimmed to two items. Three slows the reader down before the product section.
