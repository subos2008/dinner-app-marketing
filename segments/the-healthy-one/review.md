## Overall Assessment

### Top 3 Strongest Ads

1. **Live a Little — Variant A** (5/5) — The best ad in the set. "Go on, live a little" is a phrase the audience has heard a hundred times, and reclaiming it as the emotional arc gives the ad an undeniable structure. "This is what living a lot actually looks like" is a perfect close. The frustration, the understanding, and the resolution all earn their place.

2. **The Menu Minefield — Variant B** (5/5) — The best-written ad. "Your next dinner out doesn't need a contingency plan" is the line of the whole set — specific, relieving, and completely true to the segment. The body copy respects the reader's effort without patronising them.

3. **Discipline Shouldn't Be Lonely — Variant A** (5/5) — The campaign-defining hook. "Eating healthy shouldn't mean eating alone" is simple enough to work as a headline, a tagline, and a brand statement simultaneously. Everything in the body earns the opening line.

### Top 3 Standout Lines

1. **"This is what living a lot actually looks like."** — Reclaims the "live a little" dismissal into a campaign close. Works as a hook, a CTA, or a brand line.

2. **"Your macros are dialled. Your social life is in the bin."** — The spicy opener from Discipline C. Instant recognition, instant contrast. Test as a standalone hook.

3. **"Your next dinner out doesn't need a contingency plan."** — From Menu Minefield B. Names the behaviour (planning, checking, strategising before every meal out) and offers the relief. Reusable across the campaign.

### Biggest Gap

**The empathy work surfaced strong "during dinner" and "after dinner" moments that aren't fully developed in the ad copy.** The emotional journey's during-dinner scene — "Everyone orders like I do. Nobody says 'go on, live a little'" — and the after-dinner relief — "I didn't have to explain my order. That's never happened at a group dinner before" — are powerful social proof moments. Concept 7 (Nobody Bats an Eye) was developed in the concepts file but not written into ads. A dedicated social proof ad using the first-person after-dinner voice would strengthen the set, especially for retargeting people who've seen the awareness ads but haven't booked.

### Rewrite Recommendations

No ads scored below 3. Specific fixes:

- **Matched on What You Eat — Stories/Reels:** Add an emotional frame (e.g., "No more checking the menu first") between the product frames and the CTA to ground it in the audience's lived experience. Currently the weakest piece in the set.
- **Discipline Shouldn't Be Lonely — Variant B:** Rewrite the CTA. "This is the dinner you've been looking for" is generic — try "A dinner where discipline isn't the odd one out" or "You built the routine. Here's the table."
- **Live a Little — Variant B:** Strengthen the CTA. "Book it" alone is flat — pair it with the emotional close: "One dinner where your discipline isn't a conversation topic. Book your Wednesday."
- **Menu Minefield — Stories/Reels:** Replace "No more menu panic" with a more emotional CTA: "A dinner you can just say yes to" or "Say yes without checking the menu first."
