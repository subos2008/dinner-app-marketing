# The Healthy One

**Segment type:** Lifestyle (cross-cuts life-situation segments)

**Who:** On a health journey — trying to lose weight, following a diet (keto, calorie counting, intermittent fasting, whatever), or just trying to eat better. They want to socialise but food-centred socialising is a minefield. Every group dinner is a negotiation between what they want to eat and what everyone else is having. They don't want to be the person reading the menu for 20 minutes or saying "I'll just have a salad."

**Age skew:** 25-40

**Emotional state:** Motivated but socially frustrated. They're making progress on their health goals but it's isolating. Friends suggest restaurants without thinking. Group meals turn into peer pressure ("go on, have a dessert"). They want to eat out without it being a test of willpower or a source of guilt. They want to be around people who understand that ordering carefully isn't being difficult — it's being intentional.

**Trigger:** Being invited to a restaurant that's going to make their diet impossible. Watching everyone order burgers while they scan for the grilled chicken. Feeling like the awkward one at the table. Or: sitting at home on a Saturday because it's "easier" to eat alone than to navigate a group meal.

**What they'd tell a friend:** "I want to go out, I just don't want to spend the whole night fighting the menu."

**Meta targeting signals:**
- Interests: fitness, weight loss, healthy eating, macro tracking, calorie counting, meal prep
- Follows accounts like Joe Wicks, MyFitnessPal, fitness influencers, healthy recipe creators
- Engages with gym, wellness, nutrition content
- May follow specific diet communities (keto, intermittent fasting, clean eating)
- Age 25-40

**Messaging hook:** A dinner where nobody judges what's on your plate.

**Why this segment matters:**
- **Budget matching solves a real problem** — budget tiers mean they won't be taken somewhere where the only option is indulgent food. Mid-range and curated restaurants tend to have better variety.
- **Dietary matching is the core product** — the matching algorithm groups by dietary preference. Someone health-conscious will be sat with others who eat similarly.
- **Underserved social need** — plenty of fitness apps and diet communities exist online. Almost nothing helps health-conscious people socialise around food IRL.
- **Cross-cuts other segments** — a healthy Transplant, a healthy Outgrower. The diet is a lens on top of a life situation.

**Priority:** Run alongside other lifestyle segments — strong targeting potential through fitness/wellness interests on Meta.
