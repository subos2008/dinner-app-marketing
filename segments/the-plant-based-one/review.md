### The One Sad Salad — Variant A

## Overall Assessment

### Top 3 Strongest Ads

1. **The One Sad Salad — Variant A** (5/5) — The lead ad. "You didn't go plant-based to eat a roasted cauliflower at a steakhouse" is the line of the campaign. Specific, funny, not preachy. Names the frustration in a way that's warm rather than militant. The ad earns every sentence.

2. **Not Vegan-Friendly. Vegan. — Variant B** (5/5) — The sharpest writing in the segment. "Your friends are being kind. The restaurant is being compliant. Nobody is being vegan." is the kind of paragraph that makes people stop and re-read. Opens with a quote the target has heard a hundred times, then reframes it completely.

3. **The Whole Menu Is Yours — Variant A** (5/5) — The aspirational counterweight. Where the Sad Salad names the pain, this delivers the promise. "The mushroom thing at the bottom of page four that the kitchen makes when someone like you shows up" is the best single image in the segment.

### Top 3 Standout Lines

1. **"Your friends are being kind. The restaurant is being compliant. Nobody is being vegan."** — Three sentences, escalating precision. Says something she's felt a hundred times but never articulated. Reusable as a hook, body copy, or standalone.

2. **"Stop performing. Start eating."** — Two words each, perfect parallel. Works as a CTA, a campaign tagline, or a Reel closer. The word "performing" is doing all the emotional work — it captures the exhaustion of pretending the cauliflower is fine.

3. **"The restaurant isn't vegan-friendly. It's vegan. The table isn't mixed. It's matched."** — Two parallel reversals that double as positioning and as something people would share. This is the product truth in its most compressed, repeatable form.

### Biggest Gap

**The empathy work's strongest emotional beat — "I just need six people and a restaurant" — isn't used anywhere in the finished ads.** That line (from the day-in-the-life closer) is the simplest, most human expression of the desire: not a movement, not a crusade, just a table. It could anchor a standalone ad or serve as the closing line for the campaign. Separately, the "eating dinner twice" angle (Concept 5) has a lot of narrative potential that's only lightly referenced in Sad Salad Variant A — a dedicated ad exploring the absurdity of going to a restaurant and still having to eat at home could be a strong second-wave piece, particularly for Reels.

### Rewrite Recommendations

No ads scored below 3. Minor fixes:

- **One Sad Salad — Variant B:** Tighten the product paragraph — "curated, quality-checked, 4.3+ on Google" is accurate but reads like a feature list. Pick one proof point and cut the rest.
- **Explaining Tax — Variant A:** Trim the quote stack from five to three. The strongest are "Can you eat bread?", "More for me then!", and the chips line. The others dilute the impact.
- **Explaining Tax — Variant B:** Change the CTA from "Your Wednesday. Your food. Your people." to avoid duplication with The Whole Menu Variant B. Consider "The best part isn't the food. It's not having to explain the food." as the closer instead.
- **One Sad Salad — Stories/Reels:** Replace the CTA with "Stop performing. Start eating." to carry the strongest line from Variant C into the Reel format.
- **Not Vegan-Friendly. Vegan. — Variant A:** Test the closing line ("The restaurant isn't vegan-friendly. It's vegan. The table isn't mixed. It's matched.") as the opening hook in a separate variant — it may be strong enough to lead.
