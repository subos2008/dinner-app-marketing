## Concept 1: The Wednesday Night Problem

**Insight:** From the day-in-the-life: "Wednesday night. She ate dinner alone." The entire triggering day builds to this — a Wednesday specifically. And the product is a Wednesday dinner. The symmetry is the concept.

**Emotional lever:** The quiet specificity of loneliness — it's not dramatic, it's just another Wednesday with reheated pasta.

**Angle:** Name the exact night. Don't talk about loneliness broadly — talk about Wednesday. Then offer a different Wednesday. Problem → product as the direct answer.

**Headline directions:**
- "Your Wednesday nights could look different."
- "Last Wednesday you ate alone. Next Wednesday you don't have to."
- "Wednesday night. Reheated pasta. A quiet flat. There's another version of this."

**Format:** Feed ad (the longer emotional setup needs space), also works as a 5-frame Reel

**Test:** Specific day (Wednesday) vs generic timing ("most nights"). Does naming the exact day the product runs increase resonance?

## Concept 2: The Google Search

**Insight:** From empathy map: "Googles 'how to make friends in Manchester' at least once a month, reads the same listicles, closes the tab." From the day-in-the-life: "Types 'things to do in Manchester on your own.' Hates that she typed 'on your own.'"

**Emotional lever:** The private shame of searching for something that should come naturally. Everyone's done a search they'd never admit to.

**Angle:** Call out the search. Make the reader feel caught — in a way that's empathetic, not cruel. Then position the product as the answer the search results never gave them.

**Headline directions:**
- "You've googled 'how to make friends in [city]' more times than you'd admit."
- "None of the search results for 'things to do alone in Manchester' led here. Until now."
- "The algorithm knows you're looking. We actually have an answer."

**Format:** Feed ad (hook relies on text), also strong as a Story with text overlay on a phone screen visual

**Test:** City-specific ("in Manchester") vs generic ("in your city"). Also: question hook vs statement hook.

## Concept 3: The Instagram Gap

**Insight:** From empathy map: "Posts the Northern Quarter, the canals, Ancoats bakeries. Never posts on a Saturday night." Quick mode: "The gap between the life I show people and the life I actually live."

**Emotional lever:** The exhaustion of performing a curated life that doesn't match reality. The gap between the public persona and the private truth.

**Angle:** Name the gap directly. "Your Instagram says one thing. Your actual evening says another." Then offer alignment — a life where what you post and what you live match up.

**Headline directions:**
- "Your Instagram looks like you're thriving. Your Tuesday night tells a different story."
- "POV: your feed says 'Manchester, you beauty' but your actual Friday is Netflix and a ready meal."
- "The gap between your Story and your real story."

**Format:** Stories/Reels (native to the platform the gap exists on — meta and self-aware). Feed also works.

**Test:** Direct address ("Your Instagram") vs POV format. Also: does calling out Instagram make people defensive or make them feel seen?

## Concept 4: Not a Dating App, Not a Networking Event

**Insight:** From emotional journey, consideration phase: "Not a dating app. Not a networking event. Just dinner with 5 people who were matched to be there with you." From empathy map: "Downloaded Bumble BFF twice. The idea of swiping for friends feels desperate."

**Emotional lever:** Relief. Everything else in this space is either dating-adjacent or networking-adjacent. Positioning against both creates a clear lane.

**Angle:** Define by what it ISN'T first. Clear the clutter of everything else they've tried or rejected. Then say what it IS — simply and specifically.

**Headline directions:**
- "Not a dating app. Not a networking event. Just dinner."
- "No swiping. No small talk about what you do. Just 5 strangers, 1 restaurant, matched on what actually matters."
- "You don't have to audition for friendship. Just show up."

**Format:** Feed ad (needs the space to list what it isn't before revealing what it is). Strong as a Reel with quick-cut "NOT this / NOT this / THIS" format.

**Test:** Negative framing ("not a dating app") vs positive framing ("just dinner"). Does the anti-positioning land or confuse?

## Concept 5: The Real Questions

**Insight:** From creative brief: "What do you want more of in your life?" is the single best brand line. From empathy journey: "These are real questions. Not star signs or personality types." The matching questions themselves ARE the creative.

**Emotional lever:** Surprise and intrigue. The questions we ask are unexpected — they make people think, and thinking is engagement.

**Angle:** Lead with the onboarding question itself. "What do you want more of in your life?" as the hook. Then reveal it's a question from the sign-up process. The product IS the ad.

**Headline directions:**
- "What do you want more of in your life?"
- "We don't ask your star sign. We ask what you want more of in your life."
- "The questions we ask: Do you drink? What do you eat? What's your budget? What do you want more of in your life? — Then we match you with 5 people based on your answers."

**Format:** Carousel (one question per card), Reel (question reveal format), Feed ad

**Test:** The question as a standalone hook vs the question in comparison to competitors ("They ask your star sign. We ask..."). Does the competitor comparison add or distract?

## Concept 6: The Proof It Works

**Insight:** From the emotional journey, after dinner: "I didn't find my best friends. I found proof that I can still do this." The value isn't the dinner — it's the proof that connection is still possible.

**Emotional lever:** Restoration of confidence. The fear isn't loneliness — it's that they've lost the ability to connect. One dinner proves they haven't.

**Angle:** Lead with the outcome, not the product. Frame the ad as testimony — not "here's what we do" but "here's what happened." Use the after-dinner feeling as the hook.

**Headline directions:**
- "I almost didn't go. I walked home at 10:30 feeling lighter than I had in months."
- "I didn't find my best friends. I found proof that this city has space for me."
- "Two people at my table had also just moved here. I wasn't the only one."

**Format:** Feed ad (needs the emotional arc). Stories with text overlay on a night-walking visual.

**Test:** First-person testimonial voice vs second-person address. Does social proof from a fictional persona feel authentic?

## Concept 7: The Advice You're Tired Of

**Insight:** From empathy map, Hear quadrant: "Join a running club. Try a class. Give it time." From pain points: "Advice from people who've never rebuilt a social life from zero."

**Emotional lever:** Frustration. The reader has heard all the advice and none of it worked. This ad says: "We know. Here's something different."

**Angle:** Stack the bad advice, then break the pattern. Acknowledge what doesn't work before presenting what does. Creates instant solidarity.

**Headline directions:**
- "Join a club. Try a class. Give it time. — You've heard it all. Here's something that actually works."
- "Everyone has advice for making friends in a new city. None of them have actually done it recently."
- "Your mum said 'give it time.' Your mate said 'join a gym.' Here's what actually works:"

**Format:** Feed ad. Also strong as a Reel with text stacking (advice → advice → advice → smash cut to dinner).

**Test:** Stacked advice format vs single advice callout. Also: empathetic tone vs slightly sarcastic tone.

## Concept 8: Nobody Tells You

**Insight:** Quick mode: "The thing nobody tells you about moving cities is that it's not the big moments that get you. It's not Christmas or your birthday. It's a random Wednesday." Day-in-the-life: the entire narrative is unremarkable — no crisis, just cumulative absence.

**Emotional lever:** The unsaid truth. This isn't in any "moving to a new city" guide. It's the thing people discover alone and think only they experience.

**Angle:** Open with the hidden truth. "Nobody tells you the hardest part of moving to a new city is..." Then the reveal is mundane — not drama, just the quiet. Then the product as the answer to the quiet.

**Headline directions:**
- "Nobody tells you the hardest part of moving is a Wednesday night with nothing to do."
- "They told you about the job. They didn't tell you about the evenings."
- "The hardest part of a new city isn't the move. It's month nine."

**Format:** Feed ad (the reveal needs a beat of text). Reel with a slow build.

**Test:** "Nobody tells you" as an opening formula — does it land across multiple variants? Is "month nine" specific enough to call out the Transplant or too specific?

## Concept Priority

1. **The Wednesday Night Problem** — The most direct concept. Names the exact night, offers the exact solution. Should be the first ad written and tested because it's the simplest to execute and the clearest to measure.

2. **The Real Questions** — The strongest differentiator play. "What do you want more of in your life?" is the line that separates Come Join Us from everything else. High priority because it works as both brand building and direct response.

3. **The Google Search** — Universal, specific, slightly uncomfortable (in a good way). Everyone who's moved cities has done this search. It's the most "scroll-stopping" hook because it calls out private behaviour.

4. **Nobody Tells You** — Strong emotional opener with the hidden truth angle. Good for video/Reels content where the slow build pays off.

5. **The Advice You're Tired Of** — Creates solidarity fast. Good as a second wave once the audience is defined.

6. **Not a Dating App** — Important positioning but works better as a secondary message than a lead concept. Use this in retargeting or as body copy in other concepts.

7. **The Instagram Gap** — Very platform-native for Instagram but might feel too "meta" for some. Test on Stories specifically.

8. **The Proof It Works** — Best as social proof in retargeting after initial awareness. Needs the reader to already know what the product is.
