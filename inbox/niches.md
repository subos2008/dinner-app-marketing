# Niche Dinner Types

## Overview
Beyond the standard mixed dinner, we can offer niche dinner types that appeal to specific segments. These are driven by user preferences from the question set and matching algorithm. Each niche is essentially a filtered pool — same city, same evening, but a distinct group.

## Confirmed Niches

### Women Only
- Driven by: gender group preference question ("My gender only")
- Timeleft recently launched this — proven demand
- Women make up the majority of clients so the pool should be large enough
- Could be marketed as a distinct product/experience

### Vegan
- Driven by: food type question ("Vegan only")
- Group + restaurant must both work — need vegan-friendly restaurants on the curated list
- Strong identity-based community — people will seek this out

### Sober
- Driven by: alcohol question ("No")
- Group sober people together so they can connect with like-minded folks
- Could also influence restaurant selection (venues where not drinking isn't awkward)

### Vegetarian
- Driven by: food type question (would need to add "Vegetarian only" option)
- Similar to vegan — group + restaurant must both work
- Larger pool than vegan so likely easier to fill

## Potential Future Niches
- **Cuisine-specific**: Indian night, Japanese night, etc. — would need enough demand per cuisine per city
- **Age-specific**: 20s night, 40s night — already somewhat handled by age preference question but could be explicit
- **Industry/profession**: tech, creative, healthcare — would need a new question
- **New in the city**: for people who just moved — would need a new question or profile flag
- **Singles only**: driven by relationship status — currently not used for matching but could enable this
- **Language-specific**: already handled by language matching, but could be marketed as e.g. "French conversation dinner" for learners
- **Parents**: people whose friends all had kids — a shared experience to bond over

## How Niches Work in the System
- Niches are not a separate product — they emerge from the matching algorithm based on question answers
- The algorithm groups people with matching preferences naturally
- Some niches may need a minimum pool size to be viable in a given city/week
- If a niche pool is too small, those users could fall back to a mixed dinner (with their consent? or automatically?)
- TBD: Do we market niches explicitly ("Sign up for Women Only Wednesday!") or let them emerge organically from preferences?
