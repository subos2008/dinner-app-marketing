# Marketing

### Launch Strategy

- Manchester UK is the first city
- Followed by London
- Then large cites around the USA (New York)

## Market Context
- **USA is Timeleft's biggest market**, Brazil is second — validates demand across very different cultures
- Bigger cities = more usage — theory is people are more lonely in bigger cities. New York is Timeleft's biggest city. Supports launching in major cities first.
- Worth considering when planning international expansion

## Target Demographic
- **Age: 30-45** is the initial target range
- **Women make up the majority of clients** (based on Timeleft's experience)
- Ad creative and messaging should reflect this — but not exclude men

## Why People Sign Up
- Looking for love / dating
- New in the city — don't know anyone yet
- Their friends all had kids — need new friends at the same life stage
- Want a simple, recurring plan for their Wednesday nights
- Expanding their social circle
- Just want to meet new people
- Craving human connection
- Are lonely

These are the motivations to lean into in ad copy and landing pages. Different segments, same product.

## Channels
- **Primary: Social media ads** (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook)
- Campaigns run **per-city** — targeted to people in each city we operate in
- This supports city-by-city launch strategy — we can spin up a city when we're ready and target ads there
- Ad targeting: 30-45 age range, per-city geo-targeting

## City-by-City Launch
- Don't need to launch everywhere at once
- Run ads in a new city → build up subscriber base → start running dinners once the pool is big enough
- Per-city campaigns let us control spend and measure traction per market

## Post-First-Dinner Badge
- After someone's first dinner, we send them a **physical badge** with the company name/logo
- The badge is a social signal: "I'm open to chat / meet people"
- Could become a recognisable thing — people wearing them in coffee shops, coworking spaces, etc.
- Creates word-of-mouth / curiosity ("What's that badge?")

## Invite a Friend Flow
- **Trigger**: Post-dinner — "Did you have a good dinner? Invite your friends to join us!"
- Timing: sent the day after a dinner, when the experience is still fresh and positive
- The invite flow capitalises on the emotional high after a good dinner — people are most likely to refer when they've just had a great time
- Could be email, in-app prompt, or both
- Shareable link with a personal referral code — tracks who invited who
- Landing page for invitees: social proof from the referrer ("Your friend [name] loved their dinner — come join us")
- TBD: Incentive for referrer? (free week, credit, priority matching?)

## Niche Marketing
We support several niche dinner types (women only, vegan, vegetarian, sober) — see [niches.md](./niches.md). These are strong marketing angles:
- Each niche is a distinct ad campaign / audience segment
- "Women Only Wednesday Dinners" as its own campaign
- "Sober Dinners — meet people without the pressure to drink"
- "Looking for more sober friends?" — speaks directly to people early in their sober journey who want a social life without alcohol
- "Vegan Dinners — meet your people"
- Niches tap into identity and community, which drives stronger engagement than generic "meet new people" messaging

## Key Language
- **"Strangers"** — Timeleft actively uses this word and it works. "Dinner with strangers" is intriguing, slightly edgy, and immediately communicates what the product is. Lean into it.
- "Dinner with 5 strangers every Wednesday"
- The word reframes the experience as an adventure rather than a networking event
