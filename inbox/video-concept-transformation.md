# Video Concept: The Transformation

*Source: Friend's idea, kicked around 2026-02-28*

---

## Core concept

A short video ad. Starts with a person who's a bit lonely — mild, not bleak. Then a finger comes in and taps the Come Join Us CTA button. The image bursts into a joyful scene of people having a great time at dinner.

## The hook

Before the tap: a relatable, slightly flat moment. Someone on their own, not dramatic sadness — just the quiet "this could be more" feeling.

After the tap: explosion of warmth, energy, people laughing, food, connection. The transformation IS the product promise.

## Interest-targeted second half

The joyful burst could be tailored to the ad set's targeting:

- **Sporty interests** → burst into active, athletic people at dinner, maybe post-gym energy
- **Book clubs** → burst into nerdy/cosy people, maybe books on the table, animated conversation
- **Art/creative** → colourful, expressive group, maybe interesting venue
- **Adventure** → outdoor dining, travel vibes, explorer energy
- **Sober** → same warmth and energy, no alcohol visible

Same lonely-to-connected arc, different flavour of "connected" depending on who's seeing the ad. Could ask "what kind of friends do you want in your life?" as a text hook.

## Style options to test

1. **Photorealistic** — real-looking people, warm tones, editorial feel (consistent with current creative)
2. **Cartoony/illustrated** — more playful, could be more shareable, lower uncanny valley risk
3. **Mixed** — realistic loneliness moment, bursts into illustrated/animated joy (the contrast IS the creative)

Would be very interesting to A/B test realistic vs cartoony.

## Open questions

- What format? Reel (9:16), feed video (1:1 or 4:5)?
- How long? 5-10 seconds feels right for the concept
- Does the CTA button look like our actual app or is it more metaphorical?
- Could this work as a static carousel too? (Frame 1: lonely, Frame 2: tap, Frame 3: burst)

## Addendum: targeting from onboarding data

The "what kind of friends do you want in your life?" question from onboarding (sports, book clubs, art, adventure, etc.) maps directly to Meta interest targeting. We should pull those answer categories into the ad targeting so each version of the video reaches the right people — sporty burst goes to people with fitness/sports interests, book club burst goes to reading/literary interests, etc. The creative and the targeting become the same thing.

## Addendum: Three-phase video + the "vision" angle

### Three phases, not two

1. **Mild loneliness** — the quiet, slightly flat starting state
2. **Tap Come Join Us** — the dinner scene, sitting with a group of people, connection happening
3. **The friendship payoff** — those same people now doing things together outside dinner. High fives, laughter, activity. This is the "what happens after" — dinner was the start, not the end.

Phase 3 is where the interest targeting kicks in. The activity shown matches the viewer's interests: running together, exploring the city, going to galleries, gym sessions, outdoor walks, whatever.

### The deeper insight: two sides of loneliness

Current copy mostly addresses one side: **loneliness → human contact**. That's real and it's what gets people in the door.

But there's a second side we haven't built into the ad sets yet: **people have a vision for what their life looks like with friends in it.** It's not just "I want to not be alone" — it's "I want someone to go for runs with" or "I want people to explore the city with" or "I want friends who'll come to galleries with me."

The onboarding already captures this (the "what do you want more of in your life?" question with options like Sports, Outdoors, Art, Gym, Adventure, etc.) but we haven't pulled those interest categories into the marketing copy or targeting yet.

### What this means for ad sets

This might be a new layer of segmentation — not just life-situation (Transplant, Explorer, etc.) or lifestyle (Sober, Plant-Based, etc.) but **interest-vision**: what does the person want their social life to actually contain? The segments we have address *why* someone is lonely. This would address *what they want to do once they're not*.

Not sure yet how to incorporate this structurally. Could be:
- Interest-specific ad variants within existing segments
- A whole new segmentation axis
- Just the third phase of the video creative, targeted via Meta interests

Needs more thinking. But the core insight is: loneliness → connection is only half the sell. The other half is: connection → the life you actually want.
